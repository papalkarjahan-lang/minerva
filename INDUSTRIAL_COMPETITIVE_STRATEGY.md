# Minerva Industrial — Competitive Strategy (Track B / Client 9)

Companion to `minerva_competitive_strategy.docx` (the original Trade-sector
strategy) — same standard applies: every complaint and price below is
sourced from a real public review, vendor pricing page, or BBB/Trustpilot
filing, dated where available. Nothing is assumed or invented. Where a
figure conflicts between sources, both are shown and the conflict is
flagged rather than silently resolved. Compiled 2026-09-01.

**Read `SALES_CLAIMS_ACCURACY_NOTE.md` first.** This doc tells you what to
say about competitors. That note tells you what you're honestly allowed to
say about Minerva itself.

## Part One — The 7 products a Client 9 prospect might already be using (or considering)

### Procore — the one you don't compete with, and shouldn't try to

Not public pricing — Procore's own site says cost is "an upfront annual fee
... based upon your Annual Construction Volume" and routes to sales.
Third-party estimates (TrustRadius, Projul, ScanManifold — not
vendor-confirmed) put real spend at **$15,000–$80,000+/year**, plus
**$50,000–$150,000** in year-one implementation. A separate "$375/month
smallest tier" figure appears on one aggregator but conflicts with the
ACV-quote model Procore itself describes — flagged as unverified, not used
below.

Documented complaints: a BBB filing states a customer "signed up six months
ago with promises to be up and running in 60 days but still cannot use the
software and Procore continues billing them"; another (Mission Rock) alleges
Procore's unresolved integration issues were a "material breach," Procore
declined a refund and didn't respond to escalation; BBB's own profile notes
Procore "failed to respond to 3 complaints." G2 reviewers describe onboarding
external subcontractors as "overwhelming," with smaller trade partners
reverting "to side emails and Excel."

**Fit verdict — do not pitch against Procore.** Aggregated Reddit/forum
sentiment (via connecteam.com, getonecrew.com) puts its real sweet spot at
$10M+ ACV firms — "overkill below ~$5M annual revenue... like buying a
semi-truck to haul groceries." Procore also already has real, working
telemetry (RFID/BLE/GPS material tracking via its 2024 Intelliwave
acquisition, plus telematics integrations to John Deere/CAT/Samsara) — this
is the one competitor whose asset-tracking claims are NOT hollow. **Client 9
prospects who are already deep into a Procore contract are not the target.
Client 9 prospects who were quoted by Procore and balked at the ACV model or
the implementation cost are exactly the target** — same "fresh sticker
shock" logic as the Simpro play in the Trade doc.

### HammerTech — free to subs, but you can't buy it as a sub

No public pricing (vendor requires a custom quote). A third-party figure of
"$89/user/month" appears on one aggregator but **conflicts** with
HammerTech's own description of itself as a flat-fee, unlimited-user model —
flagged, not used. Confirmed vendor detail: HammerTech is free for the
subcontractor's own users — the GC/owner buys the license and covers all
subs under it. A subcontractor cannot buy HammerTech standalone.

Documented complaints (Capterra): "There is not an app available... very
important for our team to do inspections in offline mode" (Arturo V., QC
Manager); "the software for inspection is extremely slow" (Jude B.); "the
system crashes quite a lot" and bulk-import of workers isn't supported —
"you have to import one at a time" (Caroline R., HSEQ Manager); a 2026
Capterra summary adds "onboarding subcontractors is cumbersome."

**Fit verdict — this is the strongest structural argument in the whole
industrial pitch.** G2's own segment data on HammerTech's actual buyer base:
16% small business, 60% mid-market, 24% enterprise — it is a GC-tier
product, not something a subcontractor chooses. Any Client 9 prospect is, by
definition, either not on a job that provides HammerTech, or is a
subcontractor who wants their OWN record of what happened — independent of
whichever GC's system they're a guest user in on any given job. **"You don't
own that data. If you're not a HammerTech GC's guest this month, you have
nothing." is a legitimate, sourced angle.**

### Assignar — the closest real competitor, with almost no public information

No public pricing (vendor: "book a demo to get a custom quote"). A "$99/user/
month" figure circulates on comparison sites but is not sourced to
Assignar's own site — unverified, flagged.

Documented complaints (G2/Capterra): "Terrible customer support and lack of
ownership" — a promised accounting integration "never successfully
completed," and the company "refused to provide a refund" (Jillian K.,
2022); "the transition from old to new was hard" (Justin C., 2022) and
"difficult... implementing it at the beginning" (Briana M., 2023); "constant
complaints daily from employees" about app glitches and inability to see
site plans on-site (Rachel M., Owner, 2024); no support for custom forms
like SWMS, and "data is locked and can not be changed if error has been
made" (David A., 2023).

**Fit verdict — treat as the real competitor, not a strawman.** Assignar
markets directly to construction/field-ops teams (800+ customers claimed)
and is the only product in this set that could plausibly be in front of the
same buyer as Client 9. Its documented weaknesses — implementation friction,
locked/uneditable data, no refund on failed integration — map directly onto
Minerva's $0-setup, no-contract positioning. Its small-business fit
couldn't be confirmed either way from public sources — don't claim
certainty on this one, just don't concede it either.

### Raken — proof small crews already buy this category, and where it's weak

Vendor requires custom quote; third-party sources (Workyard, ITQlick,
Contractor ToolStack — consistent across three independent sites, though not
a direct vendor-page pull) report list pricing: **Basic $12/user/month,
Professional $30/user/month, Performance $37/user/month.**

Documented complaints (Capterra, 2024–2025): "cost matters to us probably
more than a larger company, so the price was what made us take a pause" —
Marika O., Admin Executive (Oct 2024); "Lacks osha part for JSS and PPE,"
no change orders/drawings, can't "collaborate with subcontractors" —
Kourtany O., Safety Coordinator, **at a 2–10 employee construction
company** (Oct 2024); "can only add equipment by hours," limiting tracking
— Jenna G. (Apr 2024). Per Raken's own help center, **Safety & QC features,
including Incident Tracking, are not available on the Basic tier at all —
only on the top Performance tier.**

**Fit verdict — the single most useful data point in this whole doc.** A
2–10 person construction company is a confirmed real Raken customer, which
proves Client 9's target buyer already spends money in this category — this
isn't a hypothetical market. And Raken's own tier-gating is the exact gap
Minerva doesn't have: **safety incident logging on Raken requires paying for
the top tier; on Minerva it's in the base product.** Equipment tracking on
Raken is hours-only, manually entered — no different from Minerva's honest
current state (manual/CSV asset register), so don't oversell an advantage
Minerva doesn't actually have there.

### SafetyCulture (Mitti) — good free tier, wrong shape of product

Confirmed current vendor pricing (mitti.com/pricing, fetched directly):
**Free** — $0, up to 10 seats. **Premium** — $24/seat/month annual ($29
monthly), unlimited seats. **Enterprise** — custom.

Documented complaints (Capterra): "the cost per person and limited use from
the any other seat" (Pete N.); "lack of customer support," unanswered
tickets (Paul O., Head of Safety EMEA, Construction); onboarding "bulky,"
users sometimes routed to wrong regional servers (Ted H.); sync delays "up
to one hour" (Ashley D.). A competitor comparison page (maptrack.com — note:
competitor-authored, treat with appropriate skepticism) claims SafetyCulture
"does not offer dedicated inventory workflows" for consumables/spare parts —
directionally consistent with SafetyCulture's own inspection-first
marketing, but this specific claim is sourced to a rival vendor, not an
independent reviewer.

**Fit verdict — real but adjacent.** SafetyCulture is a general
inspection/audit platform used across manufacturing, food service, and
retail — most of the negative reviews above are from those sectors, not
construction. It does have real asset telemetry via connected telematics
devices. It is a viable prospect's "already has something" objection, but
it's not construction-specific, and its consumables gap (per the competitor
claim above) is a legitimate, if lightly-sourced, angle.

### Fieldwire (by Hilti) — cheap at 5 users, expensive fast, no safety/asset layer at all

Third-party-reported (Capterra, ScanManifold — consistent, not a direct
vendor-page pull this session): **Basic** free up to 5 users; **Pro** $39/
user/month annual ($54 monthly); **Business** $59/user/month annual ($74
monthly); **Business Plus** $89/user/month annual ($104 monthly).

Documented complaints (Capterra): "getting a little expensive" past a
handful of accounts (Marcellous W.); "very expensive when you have more
than 5 users" (Jonathan M., Project Manager); "odd pricing structure"
(Jeremy S.); RFIs can't be created in-app and it's "unsuitable for bidding/
estimating" (Jonathan M.); sync between phone and computer "sometimes slow"
(Brad N., Superintendent). An aggregator summary of G2/Capterra data adds:
"no AI-powered alerting, no built-in financial tools, and no estimating
capability across any tier."

**Fit verdict — genuine head-to-head on price, but zero overlap on
features.** Fieldwire's own analyst-described sweet spot is "5–50 person
field teams" (contractortoolstack.com) — squarely Client 9's buyer. But
Fieldwire is plan-viewing and punch-lists, not safety/asset/consumables —
it has **no safety incident tracking, no equipment telemetry, no
consumables tracking, and no client verification package**, per every
source checked. If a prospect already has Fieldwire, the honest pitch is
"keep it for drawings, Minerva is what's missing," not a replacement play.

### GoCanvas — the one with a real, documented trust problem

Confirmed directly from vendor's current pricing page (gocanvas.com/pricing,
3-user minimum, billed annually): **Essentials** $29/user/month, **Pro**
$39/user/month, **Max** $49/user/month. An older third-party figure ($49/
$79) conflicts with this — the vendor's own current page is treated as
ground truth here, but note pricing may shift.

Trustpilot rating: **"Poor," 2.0/5, 13 reviews** — notably weaker than every
other product in this set. Documented complaints: pricing jumped "from $0 to
$1,350" annually after a free tier was discontinued (GL Paez, Sept 2023);
12-month minimum contracts with **no ability to reduce seat count** —
"every time a customer adds a seat, GoCanvas raises their contract and
won't allow them to lower anything until the contract ends" (multiple
reviewers); one user's "plan increased by 370% without authorization" (Dan
MacMillan, Aug 2025); a reviewer who blocked payment at their bank reported
GoCanvas "still took payment... without consent"; support unreachable "after
4 days" (Smoothe Denver, Nov 2024). BBB: not accredited, "failed to respond
to 1 complaint."

**Fit verdict — this is Minerva's cleanest, most sourced competitive kill
shot in the industrial doc.** GoCanvas's documented, dated, multi-reviewer
complaint pattern — contracts that only go up, never down, as headcount
changes — is the *exact* pain a subcontractor with a fluctuating crew size
feels hardest. Minerva's flat per-site pricing (Part Three below) is a
direct, honest answer to a real, cited failure mode, not a hypothetical one.

## Part Two — What the research confirms Minerva can honestly claim

Checked specifically because they're the features Client 9's pitch leans
on. Across all 7 products:

- **Real-time equipment telemetry**: only Procore (via Intelliwave/Samsara/
  CAT/John Deere) and SafetyCulture (via connected telematics hardware) have
  this, confirmed. The other five do not — Raken explicitly limits equipment
  tracking to manually-entered hours. **Minerva's honest position (manual/
  CSV entry today, real integration only once a client's hardware/vendor
  feed is connected) is not behind the market — it's roughly where 5 of 7
  competitors already sit.**
- **Worker-equipment proximity safety alerting (the "Warden")**: no evidence
  this exists as a shipped feature in ANY of the 7 products researched.
  Procore has RFID/BLE for material/asset location, not worker-proximity
  safety alerting. **This is a genuinely open gap in the researched
  competitive set** — say "as far as we've been able to find, nobody else
  is doing this specific thing" rather than "nobody else has this," since
  absence of evidence isn't the same as proof of absence.
- **Safety incident logging in the base tier**: Raken gates this behind its
  most expensive plan. HammerTech implies it exists but it's GC-only, not
  sub-purchasable. **Minerva includes it in the base tier — a real,
  sourced differentiator against Raken specifically.**
- **Consumables/low-stock alerts**: no vendor in this set was confirmed to
  have automated low-stock alerting. GoCanvas has manual form-based
  inventory checkout, not alerts.
- **Auto-assembled client verification package**: no evidence any of the 7
  products has this as a named, automated feature. The closest analogues
  (Raken/Fieldwire daily reports) are manually compiled, not an automated
  sign-off artifact. **This is the single clearest gap-to-feature match in
  the entire industrial pitch — say so plainly, but only for what's built
  today (see the honesty note in `supabase_schema_delta_industrial.sql`).**

## Part Three — Market entry rules for Client 9 (same five rules, industrial-specific application)

**Rule 1 (unchanged):** never name a competitor in public marketing. In 1:1
conversations, use the sourced complaints above factually.

**Rule 2, applied here:** don't say "we're cheaper than GoCanvas." Say:
*"You've got a crew that goes from 4 to 9 people depending on the job. On a
per-seat product, your contract only ever goes up — GoCanvas customers have
filed public complaints about not being able to reduce seats even after
letting people go. Minerva is flat per site, not per head. Your price
doesn't move when your crew does."* The prospect does the math themselves.

**Rule 3, applied here:** the GoCanvas Trustpilot pattern (contract lock-in,
"370% increase without authorization," support unreachable for 4+ days) is
the industrial equivalent of the ServiceTitan/Simpro horror stories in the
Trade doc — same close, same mechanism: *"I built it flat per site
specifically because of what happens on per-seat products when your
headcount moves."*

**Rule 4, applied here:** there's no Android-demo equivalent yet (no live
hardware demo to run) — until a real telemetry integration is live, the
honest version of this rule is the live-app demo: pull up the check-in log
and safety log on the prospect's own phone, in their browser, during the
call. No download, same as Trade.

**Rule 5, applied here — with a real caveat:** the compounding-data-moat
pitch from the Trade doc is NOT yet safely portable to Client 9. There's no
live client history yet in this sector. **Don't claim it. Use the
placeholder framing in `DATA_MOAT_YEAR_TWO_TEMPLATE.md` internally, and only
graduate it to a live sales line once real `agent_insights` rows exist for
industrial clients.**

**New Rule 6, industrial-specific:** never treat Procore or HammerTech users
as switch targets — they're either the wrong ACV tier or not the actual
buyer (subs get HammerTech free from the GC). Reserve outreach for:
Assignar users (real overlap, real documented pain), Raken users on
Basic/Professional tier who've hit the safety-tracking paywall, Fieldwire
users who need what it doesn't have, and GoCanvas users near a contract
renewal or seat-count change.

### Target priority order (industrial)

| Priority | Segment | Why | Competitor most likely fleeing |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | Subcontractors on GoCanvas approaching contract renewal or a recent headcount change | Documented, dated, multi-source contract-lock-in complaints are fresh and specific | GoCanvas |
| 2nd | Raken Basic/Professional customers who've asked about incident tracking | Confirmed real gap — Raken's own help center gates safety behind its top tier | Raken |
| 3rd | Subcontractors who were quoted by Assignar and paused (no public pricing = sticker-shock-on-the-call risk) | Assignar's refund/implementation complaints are recent and credible | Assignar |
| 4th | Subs who are HammerTech guest-users on a GC's job and want their own independent record | Real structural gap — they don't own that data | HammerTech (structural, not a switch) |
| 5th | No current software (paper site diary, phone photos) | No migration friction, same "status quo" logic as Trade Priority 4 | None |
